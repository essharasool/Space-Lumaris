# Verified facts (accuracy is judged — use ONLY these; mark anything else /* VERIFY */)

## Surveyor 3 — Moon
- Landed 20 Apr 1967, Oceanus Procellarum, ~3.0° S, 23.4° W. NSSDCA ID 1967-035A.
- Launch mass 1,026 kg; mass after landing "about 300 kg" (sources say 296 or 302 — say "about 300").
- Had a surface sampler scoop that dug trenches to test lunar soil strength (soft-landing scout for Apollo).
- Apollo 12 came ~2.5 years later (Nov 1969). Surveyor 1 (1966) was the first US soft landing on the Moon, so never call Surveyor 3 "the first".
- Apollo 12 astronauts Pete Conrad and Alan Bean landed nearby in Nov 1969, walked to it, and brought its TV camera and other parts back to Earth. The camera is on loan to the National Air and Space Museum.
- Say "the only robot explorer astronauts have visited on another world" only with /* VERIFY */ until checked.
- Photo AS12-48-7133: captions disagree (camera close-up vs. "two U.S. spacecraft"). Look at the image before captioning.
- Sources: https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/imgcat/html/object_page/a12_h_48_7133.html · https://science.nasa.gov/surveyor-3 · https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/history/alsj/a12/a12.surveyor.html

## Sojourner — Mars (Mars Pathfinder)
- Landed 4 Jul 1997, Ares Vallis, 19.33° N, 33.55° W (NSSDCA; early reports said 19.13 N, 33.22 W). Lander renamed Carl Sagan Memorial Station.
- First rover on Mars. (NOT first wheels on another world — Soviet Lunokhod 1 drove on the Moon in 1970.)
- Mass 10.5 kg per NSSDCA (JPL: 10.6 kg) → write "about 10.5 kg (23 lb)". Six wheels, rocker-bogie suspension, APXS instrument to read rock chemistry.
- Landed inside airbags that bounced on the surface. Planned for 7 days; worked 83 days.
- Do NOT say Sojourner proved water flowed on Mars.
- Photo PIA01122 "Sojourner Rover Near 'The Dice'" (rock Yogi in scene), sol 22.
- Sources: https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/mesur.html · https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/missions/mars-pathfinder-sojourner-rover · https://photojournal.jpl.nasa.gov/catalog/PIA01122

## Opportunity ("Oppy") — Mars
- Landed 25 Jan 2004, Eagle Crater, Meridiani Planum, 1.95° S, 354.47° E. Designed for 90 sols.
- Drove 45.16 km (28.06 mi). Last contact 10 Jun 2018 (sol 5,111) during a planet-wide dust storm that blocked sunlight for its solar panels. Mission declared over 13 Feb 2019. Nearly 15 years of work.
- Found hematite "blueberries" and other minerals that form in water → liquid water once soaked the ground at Meridiani. Do NOT say "lakes and oceans".
- Distance record: held the off-world driving record until NASA's **Perseverance passed it on 11 Oct 2026** (45.22 km). Never say Oppy "still holds" the record.
- "My battery is low and it's getting dark" is NOT Oppy's real words (Snopes: false). A reporter (Jacob Margolis) paraphrased the last data. If shown, say: "Oppy's last message was data, not words. It showed no power left and a dark sky."
- Operations: landed Jan 2004, last contact Jun 2018 = "over 14 years" (matches the narration). Mission officially ended Feb 2019.
- Mars time: a sol ≈ 24 h 40 min, so teams shifted their schedule ~40 min later each day.
- Photos: PIA22929 (last image, sol 5,111), PIA22930 (last images as dust storm darkened the sky), PIA22908 (final 360° panorama, Perseverance Valley).
- 3D model: NASA 3D Resources "Spirit and Opportunity Rover" (free, no copyright) — compress to ≤3 MB.
- Sources: https://science.nasa.gov/mission/mer-opportunity/ · https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/six-things-to-know-about-nasas-opportunity-mars-rover/ · https://photojournal.jpl.nasa.gov/catalog/PIA22929 · https://science.nasa.gov/resource/spirit-and-opportunity-rover-3d-model/
