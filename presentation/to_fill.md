## Summary (50–200 words)

```
Lumaris is an interactive 3D storybook website for kids aged 7 to 14. It tells the story of three NASA robots that were left behind on other worlds: Surveyor 3 on the Moon (1967), Sojourner on Mars (1997) and Opportunity on Mars (2004–2018). We show them as runners in a relay race. Each robot passed a "baton" of knowledge to the next mission, and at the end the baton is passed to the child.

Kids fly through one 3D world from Earth to the Moon and Mars while a narrator reads the story with word-by-word captions. They can tap each robot to see what it did: Surveyor 3 digs a trench, Sojourner scans a rock and Opportunity grinds a rock. Every date, place and number comes from NASA sources, and a Sources panel links to them.

Real mission data is often too technical for young kids. Lumaris turns it into a short, friendly story, so these "abandoned" robots are remembered for the discoveries they made.
```

## Project Details

```
WHAT IT DOES
Lumaris is a scroll-and-listen 3D story for kids aged 7 to 14. The camera flies from Earth into space, lands next to Surveyor 3 on the Moon, then travels to Sojourner and Opportunity on Mars. A narrator tells the story while captions light up word by word. Kids can choose Autopilot (the narrator drives) or Self-Pilot (they scroll at their own speed).

HOW IT WORKS
- Tap a robot to see its real job: Surveyor 3 digs a trench in the lunar soil, Sojourner scans a rock with its APXS instrument, and Opportunity grinds a rock with its tool.
- "Field Notes" cards show short, kid-friendly facts for each robot.
- A strip at the top shows only real mission data: landing dates, places, coordinates and sol counts.
- The story ends in a Mars dust storm, the one that ended Opportunity's mission in 2018, and then the baton passes to the child. Kids can make and download their own relay badge. The badge is drawn in the browser and never sent anywhere.
- A Sources panel links to every NASA page we used.

BENEFITS AND IMPACT
Mission pages are written for adults. Lumaris gives the same facts as a story with voice, motion and things to tap. Younger kids (7+) can follow the voice and pictures. Older kids (up to 14) get real dates, coordinates and sol counts they can look up themselves. The goal is that kids remember these robots and want to learn more about space and STEM.

TOOLS AND LANGUAGES
JavaScript, HTML and CSS. React 19, Vite, Three.js with React Three Fiber and Drei (3D), GSAP (animation), Lenis (smooth scrolling), Tailwind CSS, lucide-react icons and the Fraunces and Atkinson Hyperlegible fonts. Hosted on Vercel. The Opportunity 3D model and the Moon texture come from NASA. Surveyor 3, Sojourner, the rocket and the explorer are simple 3D models built in code for this project. All sound effects are made in code with the Web Audio API.

WHAT MAKES IT CREATIVE
The whole story is one continuous 3D world instead of separate pages. The "relay race" idea links three missions across 37 years. The light, sky and colors change as you travel from Earth to the Moon to Mars.

WHAT WE CONSIDERED
- Accuracy: we only used facts we could check on NASA pages, and we avoided common myths. For example, Opportunity's "my battery is low" line is not a real quote, so we don't use it.
- Safety: no faces, names or voices of anyone under 18 in the site. The explorer always wears a mirrored helmet, and the narrator is an AI voice.
- Performance: it is built to run on cheap phones and respects "reduced motion" settings.
- Honesty: every AI-generated image is labelled, and AI use is listed on the site.
```
